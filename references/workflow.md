# The workflow, in detail

Nine steps. Do not skip step 6 (approval) or step 9 (report). Steps 2, 3 and 5
are where the quality comes from, so give them time.

## 1. Understand the goal

Before writing anything, settle:

- **Which platform(s)?** Each one is a different format and audience.
- **Who is the audience?** Job title, interests, pain points, language.
- **What is the objective?** Reach, comments, clicks, sign-ups, sales, hiring.
- **What is the deadline or posting window?**
- **What is the raw material?** A new idea, or something to repurpose.

If the user is vague, make a sensible assumption, state it in one line, and
proceed. Ask only when a wrong guess would waste real work.

## 2. Research

Do this before drafting; it is what separates a good post from a generic one.

- **Trends and hooks** - what is working in the niche right now (web search,
  platform search, any research connector).
- **Competitors and peers** - read their top posts; note the hook, the format,
  the length, the CTA.
- **Keywords and topics** - the phrases the audience actually uses.
- **Your own data** - if a connector can read past performance, use it. Their
  best posts are the best template.

Keep research notes short. You are looking for 2-3 angles that fit the user's
pillars in `knowledge/content-pillars.md`.

## 3. Plan

For a **calendar**: one row per post - date/time, platform, pillar, format,
hook, CTA, status. Put it in a sheet or a table the user can see.

For a **single post**: a short brief - platform, pillar, hook, structure, CTA,
target length, media, posting time.

## 4. Draft

Write in the user's voice (`knowledge/brand-voice.md`), formatted for the
platform (`references/platform-playbook.md`). One idea per post. A strong hook
in the first line, because that is all the feed shows.

## 5. Humanise

Remove the tells that make text read as machine-written:

- No "In today's fast-paced world", "Let's dive in", "game-changer", "delve".
- Vary sentence length. Avoid stacked triads and identical list rhythms.
- Cut filler adverbs and hedging.
- Prefer plain words and concrete specifics (a number, a name, a moment).
- Read it aloud in your head; if no one would say it, rewrite it.

## 6. Review with the user

Show the draft (and the plan, for a calendar). Ask for a yes or edits. Do not
move to publishing until you have a clear approval for that specific content.

## 7. Schedule or publish

Through the connected connector only. Confirm the exact time, platform and text
once more. After it is queued or posted, tell the user what happened and where.

## 8. Engage

Where the connector allows, watch for comments and DMs and draft replies in the
user's voice. Reply fast - early engagement compounds. Get approval before
sending anything on the user's behalf.

## 9. Analyse and report

Pull the numbers that the connector exposes (reach, impressions, engagement,
clicks, followers). Report:

- What performed best, and the most likely reason.
- What underperformed, and what to change.
- 1-3 concrete recommendations for the next round.
- Be honest about gaps: if the connector cannot give a number, say so.

Then update `knowledge/past-posts.md` with the winners and the lessons.
